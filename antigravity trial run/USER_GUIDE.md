# 📖 INQUIRY OT Tutor - Complete Student User Guide

Welcome to **INQUIRY OT Tutor**! This guide is written in plain, friendly English to help you make the most of every learning mode, master the clinical reasoning pathway, and prepare for university theory and viva exams.

## 🧭 The Core Philosophy: The OT Clinical Reasoning Pathway

Every master occupational therapist evaluates clients through a logical sequence. Never skip directly from disease to treatment! Always follow these 8 stages:

```
┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│             THE MGRU OT CLINICAL REASONING PATHWAY          │
└─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
  1. CONDITION
     Medical diagnosis, onset, stage, pathology (e.g. Left MCA Stroke)
       ↓
  2. IMPAIRMENT
     Body structure & functional deficit (e.g. Right UE spasticity, shoulder subluxation)
       ↓
  3. ACTIVITY LIMITATION
     Discrete daily tasks the client cannot perform (e.g. Inability to button shirt, feed)
       ↓
  4. PARTICIPATION / OCCUPATIONAL PERFORMANCE PROBLEM
     Disruption to life roles, career, social identity (e.g. Unable to work as bank manager)
       ↓
  5. ASSESSMENT
     Standardized & non-standardized tools (e.g. Fugl-Meyer, MAS, Modified Barthel Index)
       ↓
  6. GOALS (SMART)
     Measurable Short-Term Goals (2-4 weeks) & Long-Term Goals (8-12 weeks)
       ↓
  7. INTERVENTION
     Frame of Reference, splinting, task-oriented practice, environmental adaptations
       ↓
  8. CLINICAL JUSTIFICATION & PROGNOSIS
     Biomechanical/neuroplastic scientific rationale and functional outlook
```

## 🎯 Step-by-Step Guide to the 6 Modes

### 1. 🧠 Inquiry Mode (Guided Socratic Learning)
- **When to use**: When learning a new concept or strengthening clinical intuition.
- **How it works**: The tutor acts like a bedside professor. If you ask about *Radial Nerve Palsy*, instead of giving a generic textbook definition, it will ask:
  > *"When a client has a high radial nerve lesion at the spiral groove, why is their grip strength weak even though their finger flexor muscles are intact?"*
- **Hints**: If you get stuck, click the yellow **💡 Hint** button or type `"Give me a hint"`. The tutor will provide a progressive clue without giving away the full answer.

---

### 2. 📚 Tutor Mode (Clear Academic Explanations)
- **When to use**: When you need an in-depth, structured review of a topic.
- **What it provides**:
  - Clear definition and anatomical/physiological basis.
  - Standardized assessment battery.
  - Step-by-step OT interventions, splints, and assistive devices.
  - High-yield clinical pearls and precautions.
  - Verifiable textbook citations (*Pedretti*, *Willard & Spackman*, etc.).

---

### 3. 📝 Exam Mode (TNMGRU University Exam Preparation)
- **When to use**: When preparing for university examinations.
- **Mark Categories**:
  - **2 / 3 Marks (Short Answers)**: Concise definitions (1 mark) + 2 key clinical points or splints (1-2 marks).
  - **5 Marks (Brief Answers)**: Structured headings (Definition, Impairments, Assessments, OT Management, Precautions).
  - **10 / 15 Marks (Essays / Long Answers)**: Comprehensive academic layout covering the entire 8-step clinical reasoning pathway.
- **"Grade My Answer" Feature**:
  1. Click the **Grade My Answer** button in the exam toolbar.
  2. Paste your own written answer.
  3. The tutor evaluates your answer against the MGRU evaluation rubric, awards marks out of 10, highlights missing keywords, and tells you what to add for full marks.

---

### 4. 🎓 Viva Mode (Oral Examination Simulation)
- **When to use**: Before hospital clinical postings or practical viva voce exams.
- **How it works**:
  1. Click the **Viva Mode** tab.
  2. The external examiner asks a sharp oral clinical question (e.g. *"Candidate, justify why you chose an Intrinsic Plus splint for a dorsal hand burn?"*).
  3. Type your response as if speaking to the professor.
  4. The examiner gives you a **Viva Score out of 10**, critiques your speed and terminology, and shows you the exact bullet points the university examiners look for!

---

### 5. 🩺 Clinical Reasoning Mode (Interactive Case Simulator)
- **When to use**: To practice real-world patient evaluations.
- **Available Cases**:
  - **Case 1**: 58-Year-Old Executive with Left MCA Stroke & Shoulder Subluxation
  - **Case 2**: 28-Year-Old Mechanic with Radial Nerve Injury post Humerus Fracture
  - **Case 3**: 5-Year-Old Child with Spastic Diplegic Cerebral Palsy
- **How it works**:
  - The tutor presents the patient's history, occupational profile, and physical findings.
  - It walks you step-by-step through the 8 stages of the pathway.
  - The live pathway ticker at the top lights up as you progress from Stage 1 to Stage 8.
  - Upon completion, the case is saved to your portfolio with your clinical reasoning score.

---

### 6. 🔄 Revision Mode (Diagnostic Weak-Area Review)
- **When to use**: When revising for unit tests or university exams.
- **How it works**:
  - The tutor continuously tracks your mistakes and low-scoring topics.
  - In Revision Mode, it presents a targeted diagnostic quiz on topics you previously struggled with (e.g., *Radial Nerve splints*, *Autonomic Dysreflexia*).
  - It displays **"Logged Misconceptions"** with the golden clinical correction.

## 📚 Master Quick Reference: Common OT Splints

| Condition | Splint of Choice | Key Mechanical Angles & Purpose |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Radial Nerve Palsy** | Dynamic Extensor Splint | Wrist 20–30° extension; dynamic elastic outriggers assist MCP/thumb extension while permitting active flexion. |
| **Carpal Tunnel Syndrome** | Neutral Wrist Cock-up | Wrist 0–15° extension; minimizes pressure in the carpal tunnel canal. |
| **Low Median Nerve Palsy** | Short Opponens / C-Bar | Holds thumb in palmar abduction and opposition for precision pad-to-pad pinch. |
| **Ulnar Nerve Palsy** | Knuckle Bender (Lumbrical Bar) | Blocks 4th & 5th MCP hyperextension (30–45° flexion), allowing extrinsic extensors to extend IP joints. |
| **Hand Burns (Dorsal)** | Intrinsic Plus / Safe Splint | Wrist 20–30° ext, MCP 70–90° flex, IPs fully extended 0°, thumb maximal palmar abduction. |
| **C6 Spinal Cord Injury** | Tenodesis / Wrist-Driven Hinge | Translates active wrist extension into passive 3-jaw chuck pinch for ADL independence. |
| **Rheumatoid Arthritis** | Resting Pan Splint | Night splint: Wrist 10–20° ext, MCP 20–30° flex, slight IP flexion; prevents ulnar deviation contractures. |

## 🛠️ Adding Your Own University Notes to the RAG Library

Want the tutor to answer questions based on your specific college lectures?
1. Open the app and click **📖 Library** at the top right.
2. In the right column, enter:
   - **Note Title**: e.g., *Unit 2 - Tendon Repair Protocols*
   - **Source**: e.g., *SRMC OT Department Notes*
   - **Content**: Paste your notes or lecture summary.
3. Click **"Index Note into Knowledge Base"**.
4. The tutor will immediately ground future answers in your uploaded materials and display citations!
